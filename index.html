Small worlds.
Real consequences.

Every simulated body attracts every other body. To make a moon orbit Earth, select Earth before adding a Moon. To fix an existing object's speed, choose its orbital parent and click “Set circular orbit.” Increasing mass changes the orbit but does not supply the needed sideways motion.

True scale uses physical radii and distances in the same units. Tiny objects have selectable markers. The optional 20× mode enlarges every surface by the same factor and preserves size ratios; collisions always use physical radii.

Contacts merge mass and momentum into a named, larger remnant. Giant gaseous objects can cross the approximate brown-dwarf and stellar mass thresholds. Neutron stars form through massive stellar core collapse, not by directly growing a rocky planet. The collapse button is an explicit simplified evolution experiment; the 2.2-Sun neutron-star limit is a chosen model assumption, not a universal measured boundary.

The Milky Way view represents its disk, bulge and halo with a sample of stars and a central Sagittarius A*. It cannot show every star, planet and comet. Use “Explore our Solar System” to resolve its planets, moons and small bodies. Distances use real AU/light-year conversions; particle brightness and glow are visual guides. Earth–Moon and Collision Lab use neutral inspection lighting so their unlit worlds remain visible. This is Newtonian gravity, not a general-relativity or stellar-structure solver.
